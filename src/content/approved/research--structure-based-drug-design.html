Designing molecules for CXCR4

Full thesis title

Structure-Based Design and Computational Evaluation of Amentoflavone Derivatives as Novel CXCR4 Antagonists

Degree and thesisBachelor's thesis
Institutionİnönü University, Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics
SupervisorDr. Seçil Demiral
AuthorUğur Cem Yıldız

I designed five amentoflavone derivatives, docked them against CXCR4 and compared their predicted ADME and toxicity profiles. I wanted to choose candidates for experimental testing.

01

Research questions and hypothesis

I examined how changes to amentoflavone affected docking scores, predicted receptor contacts and ADME/toxicity profiles.

  • Molecule design: Can selective deoxygenation and amination improve the predicted CXCR4 docking score relative to amentoflavone and Plerixafor?
  • Receptor contacts: Do the predicted poses place the derivatives in contact with binding-pocket residues through hydrogen bonds and aromatic interactions?
  • Drug-likeness and toxicity: Which derivatives have ADME and toxicity predictions that support further experimental evaluation?

Working hypothesis: I hypothesized that removing selected hydroxyl groups would reduce non-specific polar contacts and steric hindrance, lower polar surface area and improve the predicted fit around Asn43, Tyr55 and Trp104.

02

Computational method

I prepared the receptor and ligands, ran docking simulations, then compared predicted interactions and compound profiles.

Step 1

Receptor preparation

I used the human CXCR4 crystal structure 3ODU from RCSB PDB, resolved at 2.50 Å with the antagonist IT1t. I removed crystallographic waters, co-crystallized antagonists and non-protein heteroatoms, then added polar hydrogens and assigned Kollman united-atom partial charges in AutoDock Tools.

Step 2

Derivative design

I drew five amentoflavone derivatives in MolSoft ICM-Chemist. I generated 3D coordinates and minimized their energy with the NCI Online SMILES Translator, then assigned Gasteiger charges and rotatable bonds in AutoDock Tools.

Step 3

Molecular docking

I ran AutoDock Vina v1.1.2 with a grid centered on the orthosteric pocket: x=19.78, y=−6.85, z=68.11, with dimensions of 52 × 52 × 52 Å. Exhaustiveness was set to 32.

Step 4

Interaction and toxicity prediction

I inspected 2D and 3D ligand–receptor contacts in BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer. SwissADME provided physicochemical, Lipinski and ADME predictions. ProTox-II provided predicted oral LD50, toxicity class, organ toxicity, mutagenicity and carcinogenicity.

Docking results and compound profiles

All five derivatives received more negative docking scores than amentoflavone (−11.1 kcal/mol) and Plerixafor (−9.4 kcal/mol) under the same protocol. Stronger binding and greater efficacy would need to be demonstrated experimentally.

1. Deoxygenation and predicted Tyr55 contacts

Deoxygenation produced the largest change in docking score across the series. In the interaction maps, amentoflavone contacted Cys196, Asn43 and Ser459 but not Tyr55. The Deoxy pose included predicted hydrogen bonds with Tyr55 and Asp107 and aromatic stacking with Trp104.

2. Why I prioritized Fluoro-Deoxy

Fluoro-Deoxy and Deoxy both scored −11.9 kcal/mol. Fluoro-Deoxy had a predicted toxicity class of 5 (LD50: 2500 mg/kg), inactive mutagenicity and carcinogenicity predictions, and no predicted CYP2C19 inhibition. Its interaction map showed contacts with Tyr55, Asn43, Cys196, Tyr429 and Trp104. These combined predictions supported further testing; they do not establish safety.

3. The second amino group in Di-Amino

Di-Amino received the least favorable score among the five derivatives (−11.5 kcal/mol). Its interaction map showed an unfavorable donor–donor contact between the second amino group and Asn43. This contact may help explain the score in the static docking model.

06

Limitations and interpretation

How to read these results

Docking, ADME and toxicity results are computational predictions. They identify candidates to test experimentally; they do not demonstrate biological efficacy or clinical safety.

  • Static docking: Docking scores estimate the favorability of a pose within a simplified model. This workflow does not capture full receptor flexibility, solvent effects or membrane dynamics.
  • Prediction limits: SwissADME and ProTox-II use models based on existing chemical data. Predictions for new derivatives depend on how well those compounds fit each model's applicability domain.
  • Small compound set: I evaluated five designed derivatives. A larger compound library would be needed to explore other substitutions.
  • One receptor structure: 3ODU represents one antagonist-bound conformation. Molecular dynamics or docking against several conformations would help assess how receptor movement affects the results.
