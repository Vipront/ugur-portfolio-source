CXCR4 and the cancer microenvironment

Full thesis title

A Bioinformatic Investigation into the Role of the CXCR4 Chemokine Receptor in Cancer Progression and Metastasis: Expression, Prognosis, and Immune Infiltration

Degree and thesisBachelor's thesis
Institutionİnönü University, Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics
SupervisorDr. Samet Kocabay
AuthorUğur Cem Yıldız

I used public cancer datasets to examine CXCR4 expression, survival associations and immune infiltration. I wanted to see whether its association with prognosis differed between tissues.

01

Research questions and hypotheses

I compared CXCR4 expression and survival associations across breast, lung and colorectal cancer datasets, then examined genomic changes and immune infiltration.

  • Expression: Is CXCR4 mRNA expression higher in breast, lung and colorectal tumors than in normal tissue?
  • Genomic changes: Can genomic amplification or mutations explain elevated CXCR4 expression, or might transcriptional and microenvironmental factors contribute?
  • Survival: Does higher CXCR4 expression correlate with shorter overall survival in each cohort?
  • Immune infiltration: Is CXCR4 expression associated with CD8+ T-cell infiltration in tissues with different survival associations?

Working hypotheses: I tested two hypotheses: CXCR4 overexpression mainly reflects transcriptional changes rather than amplification, and high expression is associated with poor prognosis, with that relationship varying by immune infiltration in the tumor microenvironment.

02

Bioinformatics method

I analyzed TCGA cohorts with GEPIA2, cBioPortal, Kaplan–Meier Plotter, TIMER2.0 and STRING.

Step 1

Gene expression

I compared CXCR4 mRNA expression in BRCA, LUAD and COAD tumors with normal tissue data from TCGA and GTEx using GEPIA2. I used one-way ANOVA with Log2FC = 1 and p < 0.01 cutoffs.

Step 2

Genomic changes

I queried breast, lung and colorectal TCGA Pan-Cancer Atlas cohorts in cBioPortal, comparing mutation frequency, deep deletions and copy-number status with mRNA expression.

Step 3

Survival analysis

I analyzed overall survival in BRCA, LUAD and READ using Kaplan–Meier Plotter. The software selected optimal expression cutoffs and calculated log-rank p-values and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals.

Step 4

Immune infiltration and interaction networks

I used TIMER2.0 to examine the Spearman correlation between CXCR4 expression and CD8+ T-cell infiltration across several estimation algorithms. I used STRING to explore protein interaction networks.

04

Findings by cancer cohort

1. Higher expression in breast and colon tumors

In GEPIA2, CXCR4 mRNA expression was higher in BRCA and COAD tumors than in normal tissue (p < 0.01). The difference in LUAD was smaller and not statistically significant.

2. Amplification alone did not explain expression

CXCR4 mutations and copy-number amplifications were uncommon in the queried TCGA cohorts (<2%). Amplified samples generally had high expression, but many diploid tumors did too. Amplification alone therefore did not explain the expression pattern. Transcriptional regulation or microenvironmental factors, such as hypoxia-related HIF-1α signaling, may contribute; this study did not measure those mechanisms directly.

3. Longer survival associated with high CXCR4 in LUAD

CXCR4 expression was not significantly associated with overall survival in BRCA or READ (p > 0.05). In LUAD, higher expression was associated with longer overall survival (p = 0.007).

4. Positive correlation with CD8+ T-cell infiltration

Several TIMER2.0 algorithms found a positive correlation between CXCR4 expression and CD8+ T-cell infiltration in LUAD (Spearman rho > 0). Higher expression was associated with a microenvironment containing more immune cells. The analysis does not show whether CXCR4 caused the infiltration or the survival difference.

5. Predicted chemokine network associations

The STRING network linked CXCR4 with CXCL12, CCL5, CXCL10, CXCL13 and CD4. These are predicted functional associations; not every network edge represents a direct physical interaction.

06

Limitations and interpretation

How to read these results

I used public datasets and computational estimates to study associations. Establishing biological cause and effect would require further work.

  • No protein-level validation: I analyzed mRNA abundance. Protein amounts and receptor localization would need separate validation with methods such as immunohistochemistry or Western blotting.
  • Estimated immune infiltration: TIMER2.0 estimates immune infiltration from bulk gene expression. It does not count cells directly or establish how they migrate.
  • Automatically selected cutoffs: Kaplan–Meier Plotter selected expression cutoffs to separate survival groups. The association needs independent validation with a fixed cutoff, including prospective studies.
  • Differences between datasets: TCGA and GTEx differ in data processing and sample composition. Batch effects, tumor stage and treatment history may affect the results.
