Research Theses

Completed bachelor's graduation research projects in computational structural biology and cancer bioinformatics.

THESIS-01İnönü University · Supervisor: Dr. Seçil Demiral

Structure-Based Design and Computational Evaluation of Amentoflavone Derivatives as Novel CXCR4 Antagonists

  • —Rationally designed five novel amentoflavone derivatives as CXCR4 antagonists using a structure-based drug design workflow against the CXCR4 crystal structure (PDB ID: 3ODU).
  • —Performed molecular docking with AutoDock Vina. Under the same protocol, Fluoro-Deoxy-Amentoflavone received a more favorable predicted score (−11.9 kcal/mol) than Plerixafor (−9.4 kcal/mol) and was prioritized for experimental evaluation.
  • —Predicted ADME / drug-likeness (SwissADME) and computational toxicity (ProTox-II), and analyzed protein–ligand interactions with BIOVIA Discovery Studio.
  • —Docking poses suggested improved receptor complementarity after selective deoxygenation, including a predicted directional hydrogen bond with Tyr55.

THESIS-02İnönü University · Supervisor: Dr. Samet Kocabay

A Bioinformatic Investigation into the Role of the CXCR4 Chemokine Receptor in Cancer Progression and Metastasis: Expression, Prognosis, and Immune Infiltration

  • —Investigated CXCR4 expression, genomic alterations, prognosis, and immune infiltration across breast (BRCA), lung (LUAD), and colorectal (COAD/READ) cancers using TCGA data.
  • —Analyzed differential expression (GEPIA2), genomic alterations (cBioPortal), survival outcomes (Kaplan–Meier Plotter), immune infiltration (TIMER2.0), and protein–protein interaction networks (STRING).
  • —Demonstrated a context-dependent prognostic role: high CXCR4 expression correlated with improved survival in LUAD (p = 0.007), associated with increased CD8+ T-cell infiltration.
